## Directly related systems

**1. STALE** - "STALE: Can LLM Agents Know When Their Memories Are No Longer Valid?" Chao, Bai, Sheng, Li, Sun. arXiv 2605.06527, 7 May 2026. Benchmark of 400 expert-validated implicit-conflict scenarios (1,200 queries) probing state resolution, premise resistance and implicit policy adaptation; best model 55.2%. **CUPMem** is the paper's prototype: write-time "structured state consolidation" that propagates invalidations across dependent attributes (App. F.2), plus query-time constrained readout (F.3) and "propagation-aware search". Release: abstract page, HF page and search found no repo; the paper has an "Code and Dataset Access" appendix (App. G) and CC BY 4.0 license, but I could not read the URL. Follow-up work (arXiv 2608.01619, StateAuditor) reports results on STALE, so the data appear to be usable. **Overlap:** the closest published prior art. It has dependency-aware invalidation and a write-time/query-time split. **Difference:** dependencies are attribute-level, not typed justifications over an evidence log; no evidence retraction; no bitemporal versioning or crossover analysis seen (full text not read).

**2. Kumiho** - "Graph-Native Cognitive Memory for AI Agents: Formal Belief Revision Semantics for Versioned Memory Architectures." Young Bin Park. arXiv 2603.17244, 18 Mar 2026. Maps AGM postulates to property-graph operations with immutable revisions and typed dependency edges; Redis plus Neo4j; reports 93.3% on LoCoMo-Plus. Release: KumihoIO GitHub org has MIT/Apache-2.0 repos (kumiho-memory, kumiho-SDKs, Revka), but I found no repo reproducing the paper's results or AGM proofs. **Overlap:** formal belief revision semantics plus typed dependency edges for a versioned store. **Difference:** evaluated on LoCoMo-Plus, not on retraction, delayed or unreliable streams; whether dependency edges drive retraction propagation is not verified.

**3. TOKI** - "TOKI: A Bitemporal Operator Algebra for Contradiction Resolution in LLM-Agent Persistent Memory." Ziming Wang. arXiv 2606.06240, 4 Jun 2026. Frames contradiction resolution as concurrency control; bitemporal operators with soundness proofs; audit rows keep losing facts; avoids three write-time anomalies. Release: the abstract states code at github.com/ZenAlexa/toki-bitemporal-memory, but that URL returned 404 when fetched; treat as unverified. **Overlap:** bitemporal versioning plus provenance/audit. **Difference:** no retraction propagation through justification chains (per the abstract); contradiction resolution only. Note: "replay" is not mentioned in the abstract I read.

**4. Nous** - two papers.
 (a) "When Does Belief-Based Agent Memory Help? Reliability-Conditional Updating and Provenance-Capped Poisoning Defense" (v1 title: "Nous: A Predictive World Model for Long-Term Agent Memory"). Pranav Singh (IIT Ropar). arXiv 2606.22030, v2 11 Aug 2026. Bayesian per-entity-attribute belief distributions; on LoCoMo the updating is "inert" versus last-write-wins; gains only when per-source reliability tracks correctness (60-scenario contradiction benchmark); provenance-capped trust = min(source tier, content confidence) gives 0% attack success. Release: "all code and both benchmarks are public"; URL not found. **Overlap:** source-reliability-conditioned belief updating. **Difference:** reliability is a heuristic or linguistic marker, not calibrated on held-out unseen sources; no justification graph.
 (b) "Nous: Learning and Certifying Memory Decisions Before Source Calibration." arXiv 2610.00094 (Oct 2026). **Title only; the page was rate-limited and not read.** Its title suggests direct overlap with the "calibrated support on unseen sources" claim. Must be read before submission.

**5. Truth for Believable AI** - "Truth for Believable AI: Expressed Doubt, Provenance, and Belief Revision as an Engineerable Stance." Sebastian Cochinescu (Univ. of Bucharest). arXiv 2609.26035, Sep 2026. A behaviour layer over a fixed LM (tested on Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct) that expresses doubt, cites sources, and accepts user corrections only below a confidence threshold (0.75) or when corroborated by the retrieval store ("gated revision"); some pre-specified fidelity metrics failed. Release: mentions SHA-256-manifested benchmark artifacts; no URL found. **Overlap:** low; conceptual overlap on provenance-gated revision. **Difference:** conversational stance, not a belief store; no retraction propagation.

**6. Zep / Graphiti** - "Zep: A Temporal Knowledge Graph Architecture for Agent Memory." Rasmussen, Paliychuk, Beauvais, Ryan, Chalef. arXiv 2501.13956, 20 Jan 2025. Graphiti is a temporally-aware KG engine with explicit bitemporal tracking; facts are invalidated, not deleted; provenance via episodes; reports 94.8% on DMR and up to 18.5% gains on LongMemEval. **Open source: yes.** github.com/getzep/graphiti, Apache-2.0. LLM backends: OpenAI (default; Azure), Anthropic, Google Gemini, Groq, and OpenAI-compatible endpoints (Ollama, vLLM, llama.cpp, LM Studio, DeepSeek, Together, OpenRouter); works best with structured-output models. DBs: Neo4j, FalkorDB, Neptune, Kuzu (deprecated). **Overlap:** bitemporal facts plus provenance. **Difference:** invalidation is edge-local on contradiction; no justification-based retraction cascade.

**7. Knowledge-editing / finetuning baselines**
- RippleEdits: "Evaluating the Ripple Effects of Knowledge Editing in Language Models," Cohen, Biran, et al. arXiv 2307.12976; TACL 12:283-298, 2024. Data public, MIT: github.com/edenbiran/RippleEdits. Tests whether edits propagate to logically related facts in parametric models; the analogue of dependency propagation, but for weights.
- Sparse memory finetuning: "Continual Learning via Sparse Memory Finetuning," Lin et al. (Meta/FAIR), arXiv 2510.15103, 16 Oct 2025. Reports an 11% drop on prior tasks versus 89% for full finetuning and 71% for LoRA. No code link found. Follow-up: "Improving Sparse Memory Finetuning," Goyal et al., arXiv 2604.05248, 6 Apr 2026 (open-source pipeline claimed, URL not checked). Useful as the parametric-update foil; no overlap with explicit retraction.

## Benchmarks

- **LongMemEval** (Wu et al., ICLR 2025; arXiv 2410.10813). Six question types including knowledge-update; 500 questions. Public, MIT: github.com/xiaowu0162/LongMemEval; data at huggingface.co/datasets/xiaowu0162/longmemeval-cleaned (splits oracle/S/M). The knowledge-update subset tests only latest-value recall, not derived beliefs.
- **LoCoMo** (Maharana et al., ACL 2024, "Evaluating Very Long-Term Conversational Memory of LLM Agents"). Public on GitHub (snap-research/locomo, 10 conversations; license file present, terms not read).
- **LoCoMo-Plus** (Li et al., arXiv 2602.10715, 11 Feb 2026; ACL 2026 long paper per ACL Anthology listing). Public: github.com/xjtuleeyf/Locomo-Plus (data/locomo_plus.json; some generated files must be rebuilt locally; license not confirmed). Tests implicit constraints and cue-trigger disconnect, not retraction.
- None of these separates world state, observations and justified belief.

## Concurrent work found (2026)

Searches run: 14 distinct queries covering all topics suggested in the brief plus targeted title searches.

1. **MemTX: Transactional Belief Commit for Stateful Agent Memory.** Li, Wang, Lu, Chen, Li, Song, Zheng, Cai. arXiv 2607.23929, 27 Jul 2026 (v2 28 Jul). Staged writes, validate-and-commit gates, snapshot isolation, and retraction repair that "walks the retracted record's transitive descendants in the derivation DAG" with typed handling (beliefs revoked, summaries quarantined, tool actions compensated). Purpose-built 90-case conformance suite (+56 hardened) and 24-episode downstream test; five backbones. Code: github.com/lxy1134/MEMTX_ (as printed in paper; not fetched). **THREAT (high):** the same retraction-through-derivation mechanism. Differences: fixed confidence/authority thresholds rather than calibrated reliability; synthetic corruption suite rather than conflicting, delayed, unreliable observation streams; no bitemporal model or query-time adjudication.
2. **PACMI: Provenance-Aware Cascading Memory Invalidation for Long-Term LLM Agents.** Y. Wang, J. Liu, J. Zhang, Z. Wu, Y. Duan, M. Zheng, T. Cai. SSRN 7480138 (date not seen). Same group as MemTX. **Only the title and authors were seen (page rate-limited).** **THREAT (high, unverified):** the title is essentially "retraction propagation via provenance."
3. **Can Agent Memory Systems Track Evolving State?** Fan, Liu, Yang, Ouyang, Han. arXiv 2608.19652, 20 Aug 2026. StateMemBench (234 scenarios; five failure modes; current-vs-superseded grading) and StateMem (explicit dependencies and supersession). Release not verified. **THREAT (medium):** benchmark separating state tracking from observation recall; no justified-belief tier seen.
4. **A Graph-Native Bitemporal Memory Store for Conversational AI Agents.** Niksarli, Baheti. arXiv 2607.26520, 29 Jul 2026. Neo4j valid-time/transaction-time store; 80% on LongMemEval knowledge-update. Release not stated. Overlap: bitemporal versioning; no retraction.
5. **TGMS: An Agent-Native Bi-Temporal Graph Management System.** Xiaofei Zhang. arXiv 2607.10265, Jul 2026. Bitemporal store, 13 validated operators, evidence-checked answers; 0.897 on "what was believed before a correction." Code: github.com/zxf-work/tgms (Apache-2.0, per paper; repo not fetched). Overlap: belief-time queries on corrected data.
6. **MemConflict.** Tao et al., arXiv 2605.20926, 20 May 2026. Benchmark of dynamic/static/conditional conflicts across six memory systems; retrieval-ranking focus. Release not verified.
7. **When Memory Updates but Behavior Does Not (StateAuditor).** Sun, He. arXiv 2608.01619, 3 Aug 2026. Audits stored state against drafts; reaches .736 on STALE. Overlap: STALE follow-up; no justification graph.
8. **Retain or Consolidate? Budget-Dependent Operator Selection for Language Agent Memory.** Kang et al. arXiv 2607.17545, 21 Jul 2026. Budget-dependent crossover between retaining raw records and consolidating (+48 pts under tight budgets; retention +8-11% when evidence fits), on LongMemEval. **THREAT (low-medium):** an existing crossover result, though over context budget rather than write-time vs query-time LLM cost. Code not found.
9. **Fresh Memory, Stale Plans** (PlanFence; Pith lists "Derivation Currency" as title, "Dependency-Scoped Validation" in search results; authors as shown by Pith, not cross-checked). arXiv 2609.03340, 3 Sep 2026. Records input versions of plans and validates dependencies before action. Overlap: dependency-scoped staleness; limited. Low confidence in the metadata.
10. **Belief Memory** (arXiv 2605.05583; Noisy-OR belief memory on LoCoMo/ALFWorld; authors and release not read) and **Beyond Memory: Explicit Belief States** (Luo et al., arXiv 2610.01415, 1 Oct 2026). Low overlap.

Also: "Evidence Withdrawal and Belief Revision in LLMs" (ResearchGate 414863551; fetch blocked, unverified) and the survey "From Memory to Belief" (SSRN 7493158) are worth reading. No LLM+ATMS/JTMS paper surfaced.

## Implications for PALIMPSEST

The retraction-propagation claim is no longer novel as a mechanism. MemTX (2607.23929) already retracts a record and repairs its transitive descendants in a derivation DAG, and PACMI (title "Provenance-Aware Cascading Memory Invalidation"; unread) appears to do the same. STALE's CUPMem (2605.06527) propagates invalidations across dependent attributes. The "first" framing should be dropped; what remains defensible is (i) typed justifications (not just derived-from edges) over an append-only evidence log with bitemporal versioning, (ii) a measured write-time versus query-time adjudication crossover (only a context-budget crossover exists so far: 2607.17545), and (iii) calibrated support evaluated on unseen sources. Claim (iii) is the most exposed to a paper I could not read: Nous, "Before Source Calibration" (2610.00094, 1-2 Oct 2026), must be read before any novelty statement. On the benchmark, STALE, StateMemBench and MemConflict each cover part of conflicting/outdated memory, and StateMemBench separates state from observation recall, but none of the ones I read separates world state, observations and justified belief with delayed and unreliable streams and evidence withdrawal. That claim survives for now, subject to checking full texts of PACMI, StateMemBench and STALE, and the Nous follow-up. The manuscript must also cite MemTX, STALE/CUPMem, Kumiho, TOKI, TGMS and Nous, and compare against CUPMem and MemTX experimentally.


## Addendum (2 Oct 2026, after reading Nous 2610.00094 in full)

**Nous: Learning and Certifying Memory Decisions Before Source Calibration** (P. Singh, arXiv 2610.00094, Sept 2026). Theory-led: on a four-world HMM family, learning and certifying a state-decision policy needs Θ(ℓ⁻²) records while fixed-precision source-reliability estimation needs Θ(ℓ⁻⁴); a finite-sample witness certificate authorises policy replacements over channels compatible with the data. Experiments are a synthetic mutable-state ledger (45,000 held-out histories) and MiniGrid with a noisy-report wrapper. The paper states that no posterior-calibration, benchmark-gain or poisoning-defense claim follows, that natural-language extraction is not evaluated, and that no public code upload is asserted. **Implication:** RQ3 (claim-level calibration of support on held-out sources in natural-language streams) is not pre-empted. Cite it (a) as motivation for decision-relevant metrics (risk–coverage) alongside Brier/ECE, since it argues reliability estimation is the harder problem; (b) as a warning that per-source learned priors will be data-starved at 150 streams, favouring class-level priors (trusted/standard/low) with per-source priors as an ablation.

**MemTX** (arXiv 2607.23929) abstract verified directly: "retracting a belief triggers typed cascading repair of its derived records and tool side effects" in transactional, snapshot-isolated multi-agent shared memory. Contribution (2) of the proposal is reworded accordingly: the claim is retraction propagation *under automatically extracted dependencies, at matched cost against query-time adjudication, in a bitemporal store*, with MemTX-style propagation as the closest prior mechanism.
